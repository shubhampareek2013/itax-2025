## Official Provision
* Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
* Section: 28
* Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Profits and gains of business or profession)
* Effective date: As per the Act

This section provides for deductions in computing total income for amounts paid towards insurance, local taxes, rent, and current non-capital repairs for premises, machinery, plant, or furniture used for business or profession, while restricting deductions to a fair proportionate part when such assets are not wholly used for business purposes.

## In Simple Language
If you run a business or profession, you can deduct certain running expenses related to the buildings, equipment, or furniture you use. These include insurance against damage, municipal taxes or land revenue, rent paid as a tenant, and the cost of routine, non-capital repairs. If you use these assets partly for business and partly for other purposes, you only get a fair, proportionate deduction as determined by the Assessing Officer.

## Key Points
* Insurance premiums paid against the risk of damage or destruction of premises, machinery, plant, or furniture used for business are deductible.
* Land revenue, local rates, or municipal taxes paid are deductible.
* Rent paid for premises occupied as a tenant is deductible.
* Current repairs not in the nature of capital expenditure are deductible for premises (whether occupied as a tenant or otherwise, provided the tenant has undertaken to bear repair costs) and for machinery, plant, or furniture.
* Deductions are restricted to a fair proportionate part if the assets are not wholly and exclusively used for the business or profession.

## Who Does This Apply To?
Assessees who carry on a business or profession and use premises, machinery, plant, or furniture for that purpose.

## Conditions
* The premises, machinery, plant, or furniture must be used for the purposes of the business or profession.
* Repairs must be current repairs and must not be in the nature of capital expenditure.
* For premises occupied as a tenant where repair costs are claimed, the assessee must have undertaken to bear the cost of repairs.

## Exceptions
* Capital expenditures incurred on repairs are not allowed as deductions under this section.
* If assets are partly used or not wholly and exclusively used for business or profession, the deduction is restricted to a fair proportionate part determined by the Assessing Officer.

## Practical Example
*Practical Example (illustrative only):* Suppose an assessee pays an annual insurance premium of an assumed amount of ₹50,000 for machinery used entirely in their business, and municipal taxes of an assumed amount of ₹20,000 for their business premises. These amounts are deductible under this section. If a business premises is only used 60% for business and 40% for personal use, the deductible rent and taxes will be restricted to the 60% proportionate business usage as determined by the Assessing Officer.

## Official Provision
- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025
- **Section:** Section 9
- **Chapter:** Chapter II — Basis of charge
- **Effective date:** As specified for the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Section 9 establishes the scope of incomes that are deemed to accrue or arise in India. It covers income through or from any asset, source, property, business connection, or transfer of a capital asset situated in India, as well as specific streams like salaries, dividends, interest, royalties, and fees for technical services under prescribed conditions.

## In Simple Language
Section 9 clarifies that certain types of income—even if received by a non-resident or earned outside India geographically—are treated by the law as if they were earned inside India. This includes business profits tied to a "business connection" or "significant economic presence" in India, payments like interest, royalties, and technical service fees from residents or governments (with exceptions for out-of-India uses), salaries for services rendered in India, and the indirect transfer of foreign company shares that derive their value substantially from assets located in India.

## Key Points
- Incomes directly or indirectly accruing or arising through an asset, source, property, business connection, or transfer of a capital asset in India are deemed to accrue or arise in India.
- Salaries are deemed to accrue or arise in India if earned in India (including specific leave/rest periods forming part of the employment contract) or if payable by the Government to an Indian citizen for services rendered outside India.
- Dividends paid by an Indian company outside India are deemed to accrue or arise in India.
- Interest, royalties, and fees for technical services payable by the Government, a resident (with specific business-use exceptions outside India), or a non-resident (for business/earning in India) are deemed to accrue or arise in India.
- "Business connection" includes carrying out operations in India, acting through dependent agents, or having a "significant economic presence" (SEP) in India.
- Shares of or interests in foreign entities that derive their value substantially from assets in India are treated as assets situated in India under specified valuation rules.
- Fund management activities carried out by an eligible investment fund through an eligible fund manager do not constitute a business connection in India under defined conditions.

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Residents and non-residents earning income from sources, assets, properties, or business connections tied to India.
- Payers of interest, royalties, and fees for technical services.
- Foreign companies or entities whose shares or interests derive substantial value from Indian assets.
- Eligible investment funds and eligible fund managers operating under Schedule I provisions.

## Conditions
- For interest, royalty, or fees for technical services payable by a resident to be excluded from being deemed to accrue or arise in India, the debt, right, property, information, or service must be utilized for a business/profession carried on outside India or for making/earning income from an outside India source.
- For a foreign company's shares to be deemed situated in India, the underlying Indian assets must exceed ten crore rupees and represent at least 50% of the total asset value.
- Significant economic presence (SEP) conditions apply based on prescribed aggregate transaction payment thresholds or user interaction counts.

## Exceptions
- Income from the transfer outside India of foreign company shares/interests is exempt under specific conditions, such as holdings by specified foreign portfolio investors (FPIs) or minor shareholdings below 5% without management or control rights.
- Business operations confined to independent brokers, purchase of goods for export, collection of news/views for transmission out of India, display of uncut/unassorted diamonds in notified special zones, or cinematographic film shooting by eligible non-residents do not constitute a business connection.
- Fund management activities by an eligible investment fund through an eligible fund manager do not create a business connection in India merely because the manager is located in India.

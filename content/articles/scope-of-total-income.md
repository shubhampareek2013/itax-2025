## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: Section 5
- Chapter: Chapter II — Basis of charge
- Effective date: As provided for the Income-tax Act, 2025.

This section defines what is included in the total income of a tax year for a person based on whether they are a resident, not ordinarily resident, or a non-resident in India. It establishes rules regarding income received, deemed to be received, accruing, or arising in or outside India, while preventing the double inclusion of income already counted on an accrual basis.

## In Simple Language
Section 5 determines whose income is taxable in India based on their residential status:
1. **Residents:** If you are a resident, your total income includes income from all sources—whether received in India, deemed to be received in India, accruing in India, deemed to accrue in India, or even accruing outside India.
2. **Not Ordinarily Residents:** If you are a resident but "not ordinarily resident" under section 6(13), income accruing outside India is included only if it comes from a business controlled in India or a profession set up in India.
3. **Non-Residents:** If you are a non-resident, your total income includes only income that is received, deemed to be received, accruing, or deemed to accrue in India.
4. **Balance Sheet Rule:** Income accruing outside India does not count as "received" in India simply because it appears on a balance sheet prepared in India.
5. **No Double Counting:** If an income is already included in your total income because it accrued (or was deemed to accrue), it cannot be included again just because it was later received in India.

## Key Points
- Inclusion of total income depends directly on the residential status of the person for the tax year.
- Residents are taxed on worldwide income, subject to the specific limitation for "not ordinarily resident" persons.
- Non-residents are taxed only on Indian-source income (received, deemed received, accruing, or deemed to accrue in India).
- Preparing a balance sheet in India does not trigger receipt in India for foreign-accruing income.
- Prevents the double taxation of the same income under both accrual and receipt bases.

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Any person whose total income for a tax year is being determined under the Act, categorized by their residential status.

## Conditions
- Subject to the other provisions of the Act.
- For a person "not ordinarily resident" in India under section 6(13), foreign-source income must be derived from a business controlled in or a profession set up in India to be included.

## Exceptions
- Income accruing or arising outside India is not deemed to be received in India solely because it is taken into account in a balance sheet prepared in India.
- Income included on an accrual basis (or deemed accrual basis) shall not be included again on a receipt basis.

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following is an illustrative assumption for practical understanding and does not represent legal limits or statutory thresholds.*

Suppose an individual is a resident and "not ordinarily resident" in India during a tax year. They earn foreign-source income from a business that is controlled entirely outside India. Under section 5(1)(c), since the business is not controlled in India, that particular foreign-source income is not included in their total income in India. However, any income received or accruing within India for that same person will be included.

## Official Provision
- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025
- **Section:** 26
- **Chapter:** Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Profits and gains of business or profession)
- **Effective date:** As provided in the official enactment.

Section 26 enumerates the categories of income that are chargeable to income-tax under the head “Profits and gains of business or profession”, including profits of any business carried on, specified compensations, export incentives, perquisites, remuneration to partners, and certain non-compete sums. It also specifies that speculation business is distinct from any other business, and expressly excludes income from letting out of a residential house or a part of it by the owner from business income, requiring it to be charged under "Income from house property".

## In Simple Language
This section lists various types of business-related receipts and gains that must be counted as income earned from a business or profession. It covers everyday business profits as well as special receipts like compensation for terminating business contracts, payments for government vesting of property, specific services by trade associations, export incentives, business perquisites, partner remuneration allowed as a deduction to the firm, non-compete payments, Keyman insurance policy proceeds, converted inventory values, and certain asset transfer proceeds previously deducted under specific provisions. Additionally, it clarifies that speculation business is treated separately, and that rental income earned by an owner from letting out a residential house cannot be taxed as business income.

## Key Points
- Incomes listed in sub-section (2) are chargeable under “Profits and gains of business or profession”.
- Includes profits and gains of any business or profession carried on at any time during the tax year.
- Includes compensation or payments received for terminating or modifying management, agency, or business contracts, or for the vesting of property or business management in the Government or government-owned corporations.
- Includes income derived by a trade, professional, or similar association from specific services performed for its members.
- Includes export incentives such as profits on the sale of import licenses, cash assistance against exports, duty drawbacks, and duty remissions.
- Includes the value of any benefit or perquisite arising from business or a profession, whether convertible into money or not, and whether in cash, kind, or partly both.
- Includes interest, salary, bonus, commission, or remuneration due to or received by a firm's partner to the extent allowed as a deduction under section 35(e) in computing the firm's income.
- Includes sums received or receivable for not carrying out any business activity or not sharing business rights or commercial knowledge, subject to specified exceptions.
- Includes sums received under a Keyman insurance policy, including allocated bonuses.
- Includes the fair market value of inventory converted into a capital asset as of the conversion date, determined in the prescribed manner.
- Includes sums received or receivable when certain capital assets (other than land, goodwill, or financial instruments) are demolished, destroyed, discarded, or transferred after the whole expenditure on them was allowed as a deduction under section 35AD of the Income-tax Act, 1961 or section 46 of this Act.
- Speculative transactions constituting a business are deemed a distinct and separate speculation business.
- Letting out of a residential house or part thereof by the owner is excluded from business income and chargeable only under "Income from house property".

## Who Does This Apply To?
This applies to any assessee carrying on a business or profession, partners of firms, trade or professional associations, and owners letting out residential property.

## Conditions
- For partner remuneration, it is included to the extent allowed as a deduction under section 35(e) in computing the firm's income.
- For asset transfers under sub-section (2)(k), the capital asset must be other than land, goodwill, or a financial instrument, and the whole expenditure must have been allowed as a deduction under section 35AD of the Income-tax Act, 1961 or section 46 of this Act.
- For residential house letting, the assessee must be the owner of the residential house or part of it.

## Exceptions
- Sums received on account of transferring the right to manufacture, produce, or process any article or thing or the right to carry on a business or profession chargeable under "Capital gains" are excluded from non-compete business receipts.
- Compensation received from the multilateral fund of the Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone layer under the United Nations Environment Programme, as per agreements with the Government of India, is excluded from non-compete business receipts.
- Income from letting out of a residential house or a part of it by the owner is excluded from business income and charged under "Income from house property".

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following example is illustrative only and does not represent statutory limits or rules.*
If an assessee carries on a manufacturing business and receives a duty drawback on exports, or receives compensation for the modification of a business contract relating to agency termination, such amounts are chargeable under profits and gains of business or profession pursuant to section 26. If the same assessee owns a residential house and lets it out to a tenant, that rental income is not treated as business income and is instead chargeable under "Income from house property".

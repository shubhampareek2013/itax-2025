## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: Section 6
- Chapter: Chapter II — Basis of charge
- Effective date: As per the provisions of the Act.

This section provides the statutory framework for determining whether an individual, Hindu undivided family, company, firm, association of persons, or any other person is resident, non-resident, or not ordinarily resident in India during a tax year. It outlines specific physical presence tests, exceptions for employment and crew members, special rules for individuals with income exceeding specified monetary thresholds, and rules concerning the place of effective management for companies.

## In Simple Language
Section 6 sets out the tests to figure out whether a person (such as an individual, family, company, or firm) is considered a resident of India for tax purposes during a specific tax year. For individuals, residency generally depends on how many days they spend in India during the tax year and the preceding years. For companies, residency depends on whether it is incorporated in India or managed from India. Other entities are resident unless their control and management are entirely outside India.

## Key Points
- Residential status for any person in a tax year is determined according to this section.
- An individual is resident if present in India for 182 days or more in the tax year, or for 60 days in that year plus 365 days or more in the preceding four years.
- Specific exceptions apply to Indian citizens who leave India as crew members of Indian ships or for employment outside India, modifying the 60-day rule.
- Citizens of India or persons of Indian origin coming on a visit to India face a modified 120-day threshold instead of 60 days if their total income (excluding foreign sources) exceeds fifteen lakh rupees.
- An Indian citizen with total income exceeding fifteen lakh rupees (excluding foreign sources) who is not liable to tax in any other country due to domicile or residence is deemed to be a resident in India.
- HUFs, firms, and associations of persons are resident unless their control and management are situated wholly outside India.
- A company is resident if it is an Indian company or has its place of effective management in India.
- A person resident for one source of income is deemed resident for all sources.
- Specifies conditions under which a person is treated as "not ordinarily resident" in India.

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Individuals
- Hindu undivided families (HUFs)
- Firms
- Associations of persons
- Companies
- Every other person

## Conditions
- For standard individual residency: Presence in India for 182 days or more, or 60 days plus 365 days across the preceding four tax years.
- For citizen/origin visitors with total income (excluding foreign sources) exceeding fifteen lakh rupees: Presence of 120 days or more instead of 60 days.
- For deemed residency of Indian citizens: Total income exceeding fifteen lakh rupees (excluding foreign sources) and not liable to tax abroad due to domicile or residence.
- For companies: Must be an Indian company or have its place of effective management in India, where key management and commercial decisions are in substance made.

## Exceptions
- The 60-day plus 4-year test does not apply to an Indian citizen who leaves India as a crew member of an Indian ship or for employment outside India.
- The 60-day plus 4-year test also does not apply to citizen or origin individuals coming on a visit to India, subject to the income threshold rules under sub-section (5).
- Deemed resident provisions do not apply to individuals who are already resident in India under sub-sections (2) to (6).

## Practical Example
- **Example Scenario:** Suppose an individual stays in India for 180 days in a tax year and has spent over 400 days in the preceding four years. Under the primary conditions, because their stay reaches 60 days in the tax year and 365 days in the preceding four years, they would be evaluated as a resident unless an exception applies. (This is an illustrative assumption for clarity and not a statutory limit).

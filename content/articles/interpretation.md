## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: 25
- Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Income from house property)
- Effective date: As enacted in the Income-tax Act, 2025

Section 25 provides that for the purposes of sections 20 to 24, the term "owner" in relation to a property or any part thereof includes specific categories of individuals and persons who may not be the registered legal owners under standard conveyance deeds. It extends ownership status to certain transferors who transfer property without adequate consideration, holders of impartible estates, allottees or lessees of co-operative societies or companies under house building schemes, persons in possession under part performance of contracts, and holders of certain long-term rights or arrangement-based enjoyment rights in buildings.

## In Simple Language
Ordinarily, the registered owner of a building is treated as its owner for tax purposes. However, Section 25 expands this definition. It clarifies that for calculating income from house property, certain other people—such as someone who transfers property to a spouse or minor child for inadequate consideration, a member of a housing cooperative who gets a building allotted, or a person holding property under specific long-term leases or contract possessions—are also treated as the "owner" and must pay tax on the income derived from it.

## Key Points
- Applies specifically for the purposes of sections 20 to 24.
- Includes individuals who transfer property without adequate consideration to a spouse (except under an agreement to live apart) or a minor child (other than a married daughter).
- Includes the holder of an impartible estate, treating them as an individual owner for all properties in the estate.
- Includes members of a co-operative society, company, or association of persons to whom a building or part thereof is allotted or leased under a house building scheme.
- Includes persons allowed to take or retain possession of a building in part performance of a contract referred to in section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.
- Includes persons who acquire certain long-term rights (excluding month-to-month leases or leases for a period not exceeding one year) via sale, exchange, or extendible leases of twelve years or more, or via transactions, agreements, or arrangements enabling enjoyment of the property.

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Individuals who transfer property without adequate consideration to a spouse or minor child.
- Holders of impartible estates.
- Members of co-operative societies, companies, or other associations of persons allotted or leased a building under a house building scheme.
- Persons holding possession in part performance of a contract under section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.
- Persons acquiring qualifying long-term rights or enjoyment-enabling transaction rights in buildings.

## Conditions
- For transfers to a spouse, the transfer must not be under an agreement to live apart.
- For transfers to a minor child, the child must not be a married daughter.
- For lease rights, the lease must be an original or extendible lease for a term of not less than twelve years (excluding month-to-month leases or leases not exceeding one year).
- For contract-based possession, it must be of the nature referred to in section 53A of the Transfer of Property Act, 1882.

## Exceptions
- Transfers of property to a spouse under an agreement to live apart are excluded from clause (a).
- Transfers of property to a minor child who is a married daughter are excluded from clause (a).
- Rights by way of a lease from month to month or for a period not exceeding one year are expressly excluded from the rights contemplated under clause (e).
- Transactions by way of sale, exchange, or lease that do not have the effect of enabling the enjoyment of such property are excluded from sub-clause (ii) of clause (e).

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following is an illustrative assumption and not a legal limit.*
Suppose an individual transfers a residential apartment to their minor son without receiving adequate financial consideration. Even though the minor son’s name might be entered in official records, under clause (a) of Section 25, the individual transferring the property continues to be treated as the "owner" for the purpose of computing income from house property under sections 20 to 24.

## Practical Notes
*AI-assisted commentary — not statutory text or legal advice.*
Section 25 is an anti-avoidance and clarity-enhancing provision designed to ensure that the person who effectively controls, enjoys, or has been granted specific long-term rights over a building bears the tax liability under "Income from house property", preventing manipulation through nominal or split ownership structures. Practitioners should carefully examine lease tenures, cooperative housing allocations, and gratuitous family transfers against the specific wording of clauses (a) through (e).

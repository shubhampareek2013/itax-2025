## Official Provision
* Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
* Section: Section 19
* Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Salaries)
* Effective date: Not specified in official text

Section 19 provides for the computation of income chargeable under the head "Salaries" after making specific deductions for various sums such as employment tax, standard deduction, gratuity, pension commutation, retrenchment compensation, voluntary retirement compensation, and leave encashment. It outlines the specific limits and conditions applicable to each type of deduction through a detailed table and subsequent sub-sections.

## In Simple Language
When calculating how much of an employee's salary is taxable, the law allows certain deductions to be subtracted from the gross salary. Section 19 lists these allowable deductions, which include standard deductions, taxes paid on employment, and specific retiral benefits like gratuity, commuted pensions, retrenchment compensation, voluntary retirement benefits, and leave salary encashment, subject to specified limits and conditions.

## Key Points
* Employment tax paid under Article 276(2) of the Constitution is fully deductible.
* Standard deduction is available at ₹75,000 (where income-tax is computed under section 202(1)) or ₹50,000 in any other case, subject to the salary limit.
* Gratuity deductions vary depending on whether it is death-cum-retirement gratuity, defense service gratuity, gratuity under the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972, or other gratuities.
* Pension commutation payments are fully or partially deductible based on the governing rules and employer categories.
* Retrenchment compensation and voluntary retirement benefits qualify for deductions subject to prescribed statutory minimums and limits.
* Leave salary encashment has distinct deduction rules depending on whether the employee belongs to the government or a non-government sector.

## Who Does This Apply To?
This section applies to assessees earning income chargeable under the head "Salaries", including government employees, defense personnel, workmen, and employees of various corporate bodies, local authorities, universities, and co-operative societies.

## Conditions
* Gratuity deductions under serial number 6 are subject to aggregate lifetime limits reduced by exemptions or deductions allowed in earlier tax years.
* For gratuity and leave salary calculations (serial numbers 6 and 14), "Salary" includes dearness allowance if the terms of employment so provide, but excludes all other allowances and perquisites.
* Voluntary retirement deductions under serial number 12 are subject to guidelines such as economic viability, are restricted to one tax year, and are barred if relief under section 157 has been allowed.
* Leave salary encashment for non-government employees is limited by earned leave entitlement not exceeding thirty days for every year of actual service, alongside other specified caps.

## Exceptions
* Death-cum-retirement gratuity, retirement gratuity under the Pension Code for defense services, and payments in commutation of pension received under civil pension rules or specific funds are deductible in their entire amounts.
* Cash equivalent of leave salary received by Central or State Government employees at retirement is deductible in its entirety.

## Practical Example
*Note: The following amounts are illustrative assumptions only and do not represent statutory limits.*
Suppose an employee earns a salary and pays an employment tax of ₹2,500 under Article 276(2) of the Constitution. Under Section 19, the entire ₹2,500 employment tax is deductible from the salary income. If the same employee is eligible for a standard deduction of ₹75,000 under section 202(1), that amount is also deducted, reducing the taxable salary base accordingly.

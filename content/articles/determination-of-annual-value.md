## Official Provision
- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025
- **Section:** Section 21
- **Chapter:** Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Income from house property)
- **Effective date:** As enacted for the Income-tax Act, 2025 (with amendment by the Finance Act, 2026, w.e.f. 1-4-2026).

This section provides the statutory rules for determining the annual value of any property for the purposes of section 20, including provisions for let-out properties, vacant properties, deduction of local taxes, unrealised rent, properties held as stock-in-trade, and self-occupied properties.

## In Simple Language
Section 21 tells you how to calculate the "annual value" of a house property, which is the starting point for computing house property income under Section 20. For a normal let-out property, the annual value is generally the higher of its expected rental value or the actual rent received. However, if a property stays vacant and that causes the rent to drop below the expected rental value, the lower actual rent received is taken instead. You also get to deduct local taxes actually paid by the owner during the year, and exclude rent that cannot be realised. Special rules apply if the property is held as stock-in-trade, or if it is used for the owner's own residence (where the annual value may be taken as nil for up to two specified houses).

## Key Points
- **General Rule for Let-out Property:** The annual value is the higher of the sum for which the property might reasonably be expected to let from year to year, or the actual rent received or receivable.
- **Vacancy Adjustment:** If the property is let and vacant for the whole or part of the tax year, and the actual rent received or receivable is lower than the expected letting sum purely due to that vacancy, the annual value is deemed to be the amount actually received or receivable.
- **Local Taxes Deduction:** The annual value must be reduced by local authority taxes (including service taxes) actually paid by the owner during the tax year, regardless of when those taxes originally became payable.
- **Unrealised Rent:** Rent that cannot be realised by the owner is excluded from the actual rent received or receivable, subject to prescribed rules.
- **Stock-in-Trade:** Where property is held as stock-in-trade and is not let at all during the tax year, its annual value is nil up to two years from the end of the financial year in which the completion certificate is obtained from the competent authority (amended by the Finance Act, 2026, w.e.f. 1-4-2026).
- **Self-Occupied Property:** The annual value of a house (or part thereof) occupied by the owner for their own residence, or which the owner cannot actually occupy due to any reason, is taken as nil.
- **Limit on Self-Occupied Properties:** The nil annual value for self-occupied properties applies to a maximum of two houses specified by the assessee, provided they are not actually let during the tax year and the owner does not derive any other benefit from them.

## Who Does This Apply To?
This section applies to taxpayers (assessees) who own house properties that are let out, kept vacant, held as stock-in-trade, or occupied for their own residence.

## Conditions
- Local authority taxes must be actually paid by the owner during the tax year to be deducted from the annual value.
- For a vacancy reduction to apply, the lower actual rent must be a direct result of the property being vacant for the whole or part of the tax year.
- The nil annual value for self-occupied properties is restricted to up to two houses specified by the assessee.
- A self-occupied house must not be actually let out during any time of the tax year, and the owner must not derive any other benefit from it.
- For stock-in-trade properties, the nil annual value applies only if the property is not let wholly or partly at any time during the tax year, and only up to two years from the end of the financial year of obtaining the completion certificate.

## Exceptions
- If a property is let and experiences a vacancy resulting in actual rent falling below the expected letting sum, the annual value rule shifts from the higher-of formula to the actual amount received or receivable.
- If a property is held as stock-in-trade and meets the timing and non-letting conditions, its annual value is nil instead of following the standard letting comparisons.
- If a house is occupied by the owner for residence (or cannot be occupied due to any reason) up to a maximum of two specified houses and is not let out, its annual value is taken as nil.

## Practical Example
*(Note: Amounts and figures in this practical example are illustrative assumptions only and do not represent statutory limits.)*
Suppose an owner lets out a residential property. The sum for which the property might reasonably be expected to let from year to year is ₹2,00,000, but the actual rent received during the tax year is ₹2,40,000. Under sub-section (1), the annual value is deemed to be the higher of the two, which is ₹2,40,000. If the owner paid ₹10,000 in municipal taxes during the tax year, that amount is deducted under sub-section (3), resulting in an annual value of ₹2,30,000.

## Official Provision

- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025 (Act No. 30 of 2025)
- **Section:** 4 — Charge of Income-tax
- **Chapter:** II — Basis of charge
- **Effective date:** 1 April 2026, when the Income-tax Act, 2025 came into force and the Income-tax Act, 1961 was repealed.

Section 4 is the charging section of the new Act — the provision that establishes that income-tax is levied at all. Based on the official consolidated text, it works as follows in substance (paraphrased, not quoted verbatim — always confirm exact wording against the official PDF linked in Sources before relying on it for compliance):

- Sub-section (1) provides that where a Central Act (the annual Finance Act) enacts that income-tax shall be charged for a tax year, tax is charged in accordance with, and subject to, the provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025 in respect of the total income of that tax year of every person.
- Sub-section (2) makes clear that the charge is on the **total income of the tax year** of every person, computed under the Act.
- Sub-section (3) clarifies that "income-tax" also includes any additional income-tax, by whatever name it may be called, that is charged under a Central Act.
- Sub-section (4) provides that if the Act says income-tax is to be charged on income of a period other than the tax year, it is charged accordingly.
- Sub-section (5) provides that, for income chargeable under this section, income-tax is deducted or collected at source or paid in advance as provided under the Act.

> The exact wording of the section is shown in the “Official Text” block above, taken from the official PDF.

## In Simple Language

Section 4 is what actually gives the government the legal power to collect income-tax from you. Every other provision in the Act — how to compute income, which deductions apply, what rates apply — only matters *because* Section 4 exists. Two things anchor it:

1. Tax is charged **year by year**, and each year the Finance Act (the Budget legislation) has to say so — the charge doesn't renew itself automatically without that annual Act.
2. What gets taxed is the **total income of the "tax year"** — a term that replaces the old, more confusing pair of "previous year" and "assessment year" used under the 1961 Act.

## Key Points

- This is the charging section — the legal foundation for the entire Act.
- Tax is charged on the total income of the **tax year**, not the "previous year" (terminology used under the 1961 Act).
- The charge depends on the annual Finance Act specifying that tax is to be charged for that tax year.
- "Income-tax" is defined broadly enough to include additional income-tax "by whatever name called."

## Who Does This Apply To?

Every "person" as defined under the Act — individuals, Hindu Undivided Families, companies, firms, LLPs, associations of persons, bodies of individuals, local authorities, and every other artificial juridical person — to the extent they have total income chargeable under the Act.

## Conditions

- There must be a Central Act (ordinarily the Finance Act) in force declaring that income-tax is to be charged for the relevant tax year.
- The person must have "total income" as computed under the Act for that tax year.

## Exceptions

Specific exemptions, exclusions and concessional treatments are dealt with elsewhere in the Act (e.g. in the provisions on exempt incomes and in Schedules), not in Section 4 itself. This article will link to those provisions as they are published.

## Practical Example

Suppose an individual has total income for Tax Year 2026–27 (1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027). ## 1961 vs 2025

| | Income-tax Act, 1961 (Section 4) | Income-tax Act, 2025 (Section 4) |
|---|---|---|
| Structure | Two sub-sections and one proviso | Reorganised into five sub-sections, in simpler language |
| Charging unit | "Total income of the previous year" | "Total income of the tax year" |
| Terminology | "Previous year" / "assessment year" | Single unified "tax year" concept |
| Substance | Charges tax where the Finance Act so enacts | Same underlying charge; renumbering and drafting simplification, not a substantive change in who is taxed |

This is a drafting simplification and terminology change, not a new tax or a change in who is liable — confirmed by the Income Tax Department's own transition FAQ, which states the new Act does not impose any new tax.

## Practical Notes

*AI-assisted commentary — not statutory text or legal advice.*

The renumbering from a two-sub-section charging provision to a five-sub-section one is consistent with the drafting philosophy stated by CBDT for the whole Act: folding provisos and explanations into the main body of the section rather than leaving them as separate provisos. For compliance purposes, the practical trigger — the Finance Act specifying the charge for the tax year — hasn't changed. The main thing professionals and taxpayers should reconcile going forward is that every reference to "previous year" in old workpapers, templates and software needs to map to "tax year" under the new Act; the transition FAQ confirms these are the same underlying concept for continuity purposes.

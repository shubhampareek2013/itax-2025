## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: 23
- Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Income from house property)
- Effective date: 1-4-2026 (as substituted by the Finance Act, 2026)

Section 23 provides that arrears of rent received from a tenant or unrealised rent realised subsequently shall be deemed to be income from house property for the tax year of receipt or realisation. Such amounts must be included in the assessee's total income under the head "Income from house property" regardless of whether the assessee owns the property in that tax year, and a standard deduction of 30% is allowed against such sums.

## In Simple Language
When a property owner receives past unpaid rent (arrears) or manages to collect rent that was earlier treated as unrealised, that money is counted as taxable income for the year in which it is actually received or collected. The law treats this as income from house property even if the person no longer owns the property during that year. Out of this amount, the taxpayer is given a straight 30% deduction, and only the remaining 70% is added to their total income.

## Key Points
- Arrears of rent received or unrealised rent subsequently realised is deemed to be income from house property in the tax year of receipt or realisation.
- The amount must be included in the total income under the head "Income from house property".
- This inclusion applies whether or not the assessee is the owner of the property in the tax year of receipt or realisation.
- A fixed deduction equal to 30% of the arrears of rent or unrealised rent is allowed.
- Substituted by the Finance Act, 2026, w.e.f. 1-4-2026 (from the date of original enactment).

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Assessee who receives arrears of rent from a tenant.
- Assessee who subsequently realises rent that was previously unrealised from a tenant.
- Persons who receive such amounts, irrespective of whether they continue to own the property in the tax year of receipt or realisation.

## Conditions
- There must be a receipt of arrears of rent from a tenant or a subsequent realisation of unrealised rent from a tenant.
- The amount is taxable in the specific tax year in which it is received or realised.

## Exceptions
- No exceptions are stated in the official text.

## Practical Example
*(Note: Amounts in this practical example are illustrative assumptions only and do not represent statutory limits.)*
Suppose an individual receives ₹1,00,000 as arrears of rent from a former tenant during a particular tax year, and they no longer own that house. Under Section 23, this ₹1,00,000 is deemed to be income from house property for that tax year. A deduction of 30% (₹30,000) is allowed from this amount, leaving ₹70,000 to be included in the individual's total income under the head "Income from house property".

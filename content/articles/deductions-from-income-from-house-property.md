## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: Section 22
- Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Income from house property)
- Effective date: As provided under the relevant commencement provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Section 22 governs the computation of income under the head "Income from house property" by providing specific deductions from the annual value, including a standard deduction percentage, interest on borrowed capital, pre-construction interest instalments, and placing overriding ceilings and withholding requirements on certain interest payments.

## In Simple Language
When calculating your taxable income from house property, you are allowed to subtract certain amounts from the annual value of the property. First, you get a 30% standard deduction of the annual value. Second, if you took a loan to buy, build, repair, renew, or reconstruct the property, you can deduct the interest payable on that loan. If you paid interest on a loan before the construction or acquisition was completed, that pre-construction interest can be claimed in five equal instalments starting from the year of completion. However, for certain properties specified under section 21(6), there are strict monetary limits on how much interest you can deduct, and interest paid outside India may be disallowed if taxes are not deducted or paid properly.

## Key Points
- 30% standard deduction: Computed directly from the annual value determined under section 21.
- Borrowed capital interest: Deduction is available for interest payable on capital borrowed for acquiring, constructing, repairing, renewing, or reconstructing the property.
- Pre-construction interest: Interest for the period prior to the tax year of acquisition or construction is allowed in five equal instalments across the acquisition/construction year and the four immediately succeeding tax years.
- Computation of pre-construction interest: Must be reduced by any amount already allowed as a deduction under other provisions of the Act.
- Aggregate ceiling on specific properties: For properties referred to in section 21(6), the total deduction under sub-section (1)(b) and (c) is capped at ₹2,00,000 (subject to conditions including completion within five years and furnishing a certificate) or ₹30,000 in other cases, and the overall aggregate across such properties cannot exceed ₹2,00,000.
- Mandatory certificate: A certificate must be furnished from the person to whom interest is payable, specifying the amount of interest payable on borrowed capital and any new loan taken for repayment.
- Disallowance of foreign interest: Interest payable outside India is disallowed as a deduction if tax has not been paid or deducted under Chapter XIX-B and there is no agent in India under section 306.

## Who Does This Apply To?
This applies to taxpayers who derive income from house property and compute their total income under Chapter IV of the Income-tax Act, 2025, specifically those who claim deductions for repairs, maintenance, or borrowed capital utilized for property acquisition, construction, repair, renewal, or reconstruction.

## Conditions
- Completion condition for the ₹2,00,000 limit: The property must be acquired or constructed with borrowed capital, and such acquisition or construction must be completed within five years from the end of the tax year in which the capital was borrowed.
- Certificate requirement: The assessee must furnish a certificate from the person to whom interest is payable specifying the required loan and interest details.
- Tax deduction and agency conditions for foreign interest: For interest payable outside India, tax must be paid or deducted under Chapter XIX-B, or an agent must exist in India as per section 306.

## Exceptions
- If the conditions for the higher ₹2,00,000 deduction limit on properties under section 21(6) are not met (such as non-completion within five years or failure to furnish a certificate), the deduction is restricted to ₹30,000 in other cases.
- Interest already allowed under any other provision of the Act is excluded from pre-construction interest computations under section 22(1)(c).

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following is an illustrative example with assumed figures for educational purposes.*
Suppose an individual owns a house property with an annual value determined under section 21 of ₹5,00,000. 
1. The taxpayer first claims a 30% standard deduction on the annual value, which equals ₹1,50,000.
2. If the taxpayer paid ₹1,20,000 of interest on a housing loan during the year, and also has a pre-construction interest instalment of ₹20,000, the total interest deduction claimed under borrowed capital provisions is ₹1,40,000 (subject to applicability under section 21(6) limits).
3. These deductions are subtracted from the annual value to arrive at the final taxable income under "Income from house property."

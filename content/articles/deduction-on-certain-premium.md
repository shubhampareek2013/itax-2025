## Official Provision
* Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
* Section: 30
* Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income
* Effective date: As applicable to the Income-tax Act, 2025

This section allows specific insurance premiums paid by an assessee to be deducted when computing income chargeable under section 26 under the head "Profits and gains of business or profession." It covers insurance against damage or destruction of business stocks or stores, cattle life insurance premiums paid by a federal milk co-operative society, and employer-paid health insurance premiums for employees paid through modes other than cash under approved schemes.

## In Simple Language
When calculating business or professional income, you can deduct certain insurance payments you make. Specifically, this covers insuring your business goods against damage, paying cattle insurance for members if you are a federal milk co-operative society, and paying health insurance for your employees (as long as you do not pay the health insurance premium in cash).

## Key Points
* Deductions are allowed for insurance premiums against the risk of damage or destruction of stocks or stores used for business or profession.
* Federal milk co-operative societies can deduct life insurance premiums paid for cattle owned by a member of a primary society that supplies milk to the federal society.
* Employers can deduct health insurance premiums paid for employees, provided the payment is made through any mode other than cash.
* Employee health insurance must be under a scheme framed by the General Insurance Corporation of India (approved by the Central Government) or any other insurer (approved by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority).

## Who Does This Apply To?
* Any assessee who pays insurance premiums against the risk of damage or destruction of stocks or stores used for business or profession.
* A federal milk co-operative society paying insurance on the life of cattle owned by members of a primary milk-supplying society.
* Assessees acting as employers paying health insurance for their employees.

## Conditions
* Stocks or stores must be used for the purposes of the business or profession.
* For employee health insurance, the payment must be made through a mode other than cash.
* The employee health insurance scheme must be framed by the General Insurance Corporation of India and approved by the Central Government, or framed by any other insurer and approved by the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority.

## Exceptions
* Employer payments for employee health insurance made in cash are not allowed as a deduction under clause (c).

## Practical Example
*Note: The following amounts are illustrative assumptions.*
Suppose ABC Enterprises pays ₹50,000 via bank transfer to keep in force a health insurance scheme for its employees, approved by an insurer under the Insurance Regulatory and Development Authority Act, 1999. Because the payment is made through a mode other than cash under an approved scheme, ABC Enterprises may claim this sum as a deduction under Section 30 when computing its business profits.

## Official Provision
- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025
- **Section:** 13
- **Chapter:** Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Heads of income)
- **Effective Date:** As per the official provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Section 13 provides that, save as otherwise provided in the Act, all incomes shall be classified under five specific heads of income for the purposes of the charge of income-tax and the computation of total income: Salaries, Income from house property, Profits and gains of business or profession, Capital gains, and Income from other sources.

## In Simple Language
Subject to other provisions of the Act, every type of income must be sorted into one of five categories (called "heads of income") before it can be taxed and before total income can be calculated. 

## Key Points
- Classification of all incomes is mandatory for charging income-tax and computing total income.
- There are five specified heads: Salaries, Income from house property, Profits and gains of business or profession, Capital gains, and Income from other sources.
- The classification rule operates "save as otherwise provided in this Act."

## Who Does This Apply To?
This applies to any taxpayer whose income is being computed under the Income-tax Act, 2025.

## Conditions
- Incomes must be classified under the specified heads for the purpose of charge of income-tax and computation of total income.
- Subject to any other contrary provisions within the Act ("save as otherwise provided").

## Exceptions
- The classification rule is subject to any other provisions ("save as otherwise provided") contained in the Act.

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following is an illustrative assumption and not a legal limit.*
If an individual earns a monthly wage, receives rental income from a building, and earns interest on a bank savings account, each of these distinct receipts must be sorted under their respective heads (Salaries, Income from house property, and Income from other sources) to compute total income under the Act.

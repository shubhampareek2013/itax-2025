## Official Provision
- **Act:** Income-tax Act, 2025
- **Section:** 29
- **Chapter:** Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Profits and gains of business or profession)
- **Effective date:** As per the official enactment of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

Section 29 governs deductions allowed to an employer assessee in computing income chargeable under section 26, specifically concerning contributions made toward recognised provident funds, approved superannuation funds, specified pension schemes, approved gratuity funds, and employee contributions received under certain conditions. It also sets out restrictions on provisions for gratuity and contributions to unauthorized funds or trusts.

## In Simple Language
When an employer calculates their business or professional profits, they can deduct certain payments made for the welfare of their employees. These include contributions to recognized provident funds, approved superannuation funds, specific pension schemes (capped at 14% of the employee's salary), and approved gratuity funds. Additionally, provisions for gratuity that has become payable during the tax year are deductible, while general provisions for future retirement gratuities are restricted. Employee contributions withheld by the employer are also deductible if deposited into the employee's fund by the due date for filing the income tax return.

## Key Points
- Allows deductions for employer contributions to recognized provident funds or approved superannuation funds, subject to prescribed limits and Board conditions.
- Permits deductions for employer contributions to a pension scheme referred to in section 124, up to 14% of the employee's salary (including dearness allowance if employment terms provide, but excluding other allowances and perquisites) in the tax year.
- Allows deductions for sums paid toward an approved gratuity fund created for the exclusive benefit of employees under an irrevocable trust.
- Permits deductions for provisions made to contribute to an approved gratuity fund or for gratuity that has become payable during the tax year.
- Allows a deduction for employee contributions received by the employer if credited to the employee's account in the relevant fund on or before the due date of filing the return of income under section 263(1).
- Prohibits deductions for general provisions made for the payment of gratuity upon retirement or termination, except as permitted for approved gratuity funds or gratuity payable in the tax year.
- Prohibits double deductions: if a deduction is allowed for a provision under sub-section (1)(d), no further deduction is allowed on actual payment made from that provision.
- Restricts deductions for sums paid to set up or contribute to any fund, trust, company, association, society, or institution, unless specifically provided under sub-section (1)(a), (b), or (c), or required by any other law in force.

## Who Does This Apply To?
This section applies to an assessee who is an employer computing income chargeable under section 26 (Profits and gains of business or profession).

## Conditions
- Contributions to recognized provident funds or approved superannuation funds must adhere to prescribed limits and specified Board conditions regarding non-annual or variable contributions.
- Pension scheme contributions are capped at 14% of the employee's salary.
- Gratuity funds must be approved and created for the exclusive benefit of employees under an irrevocable trust.
- Employee contributions must be credited to the employee's account in the relevant fund on or before the due date for filing the return of income under section 263(1).

## Exceptions
- Sub-section (1)(d) operates irrespective of anything contained in sub-section (2), allowing deductions for provisions made for contributions to an approved gratuity fund or for gratuities payable during the tax year.
- Contributions to funds, trusts, or institutions are permitted if required by any other law in force, overriding the general prohibition in sub-section (3).

## Practical Example
- **Scenario:** Company A contributes 12% of its employee's salary towards an approved superannuation fund and makes a provision for gratuity that has become payable during the tax year. 
- **Application:** Company A may claim these sums as deductions under Section 29, subject to the prescribed limits, conditions, and verification of timely crediting of any employee-related components.

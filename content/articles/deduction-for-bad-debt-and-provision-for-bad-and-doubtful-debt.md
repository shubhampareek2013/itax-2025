## Official Provision
- Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
- Section: Section 31
- Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Profits and gains of business or profession)
- Effective date: 1-4-2026 (substituted by the Finance Act, 2026, from the date of original enactment)

Section 31 provides that specified assesses can claim deductions for provisions for bad and doubtful debts up to prescribed limits in computing income chargeable under section 26. It also details the rules under which actual bad debts (or parts thereof) written off as irrecoverable are allowed as deductions.

## In Simple Language
Certain financial institutions—such as scheduled banks, non-scheduled banks, co-operative banks (excluding primary agricultural credit societies and primary co-operative agricultural and rural development banks), foreign banks, public financial institutions, State Financial Corporations, State Industrial Investment Corporations, and non-banking financial companies—are permitted to deduct specific amounts set aside as provisions for bad and doubtful debts. Additionally, businesses can claim deductions for actual bad debts written off during the tax year, provided certain conditions regarding prior income inclusion or ordinary money lending are met. Special coordination rules apply to ensure that banks claiming provisions do not double-dip when writing off actual bad debts against those provisions.

## Key Points
- **Provision Deductions (Sub-section 1):** Scheduled banks, non-scheduled banks, and eligible co-operative banks can deduct up to 8.5% of total income (computed before deductions under this clause and Chapter VIII) plus an additional amount up to 10% of aggregate average advances made by rural branches. They may also opt for an additional amount relating to the income from redemption of securities under a Central Government scheme, up to specified limits.
- **Other Financial Institutions (Sub-section 1):** Foreign banks, public financial institutions, State Financial Corporations, State Industrial Investment Corporations, and NBFCs can deduct not more than 5% of their total income computed before deductions under this clause and Chapter VIII.
- **Actual Bad Debts Written Off (Sub-section 2):** Bad debts or parts thereof written off as irrecoverable in the accounts during the tax year are allowed as deductions, subject to specific conditions on prior taxation, money lending business, and coordination with the provision account.
- **Deficiency on Ultimate Recovery (Sub-section 2):** If the ultimate recovery on a debt is less than the difference between the debt and the amount previously deducted, the deficiency is deductible in the tax year of ultimate recovery.
- **ICDS Alignment (Sub-section 3):** Bad debts recognized under notified income computation and disclosure standards without recording in the accounts are deemed to be written off for deduction purposes.

## Who Does This Apply To?
- Scheduled banks (other than banks incorporated outside India)
- Non-scheduled banks
- Co-operative banks (excluding primary agricultural credit societies or primary co-operative agricultural and rural development banks)
- Banks incorporated outside India
- Public financial institutions, State Financial Corporations, and State Industrial Investment Corporations
- Non-banking financial companies (NBFCs)
- Assessees carrying on the business of banking or money lending

## Conditions
- For provision deductions, the percentage limits apply to total income computed before making any deduction under this clause and Chapter VIII.
- For bad debts under sub-section (2), the amount must either have been taken into account in computing the assessee's income of the tax year (or an earlier tax year) or represent money lent in the ordinary course of banking or money lending.
- For assessees maintaining a provision under sub-section (1), only the amount exceeding the credit balance in that provision account is allowed as a deduction, and only when the bad debt has been debited to that single provision account related to all types of advances.
- Bad debts written off as irrecoverable must not include any provision for bad and doubtful debt.

## Exceptions
- Primary agricultural credit societies and primary co-operative agricultural and rural development banks are excluded from the co-operative banks eligible for the 8.5% and rural advance provisions.

## Practical Example
*Disclaimer: The following numbers are illustrative assumptions and do not represent statutory limits.*
Suppose a scheduled bank calculates its total income before deductions under this clause and Chapter VIII as ₹1,00,00,000. Under Section 31(1), it calculates a basic provision for bad and doubtful debts up to 8.5% of this total income, which equals ₹8,50,000, alongside any applicable rural branch advance additional amounts. If the bank later writes off an actual bad debt of ₹10,00,000 and has an existing credit balance in its provision account, it can deduct the excess amount under the rules specified in sub-section (2).

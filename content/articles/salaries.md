## Official Provision
* Act: Income-tax Act, 2025
* Section: 15
* Chapter: Chapter IV — Computation of total income (Part: Salaries)
* Effective date: As per the official provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025.

This section establishes the basis of charge for income under the head "Salaries," dictating that salaries due, paid in advance, or paid as arrears are taxable, while explicitly excluding partner remuneration from firms and preventing double taxation of advance salary.

## In Simple Language
Section 15 determines when salary income becomes taxable under the law. It states that salary is taxable on a "due" or "receipt" basis—whichever happens earlier. This means salary is taxable when it becomes due to an employee (even if not yet paid), when it is paid or allowed in advance before becoming due, or when salary arrears are paid (provided they were not taxed in an earlier year). The section also clarifies that payments to a firm's partner by the firm do not count as salary under this section, and salary paid in advance is not taxed again when it officially becomes due.

## Key Points
* Salary due in the tax year is taxable whether it is actually paid or not.
* Salary paid or allowed in the tax year before it becomes due is taxable.
* Arrears of salary paid or allowed in the tax year are taxable if not charged to tax in an earlier year.
* For the purposes of sub-section (1), an employer includes a former employer.
* Salary paid in advance that is included in total income for a tax year shall not be included again when it eventually becomes due.
* Any salary, bonus, commission, or remuneration due to or received by a partner from a firm is not regarded as salary under this section.

## Who Does This Apply To?
This applies to any assessee who earns income chargeable under the head "Salaries" from an employer or former employer, but explicitly excludes partners of a firm receiving remuneration from that firm from this specific categorization.

## Conditions
* The salary must be due from an employer to an assessee in the tax year (whether paid or not).
* The salary must be paid or allowed to the assessee in the tax year by or on behalf of an employer though not due or before it became due.
* Arrears of salary must be paid or allowed in the tax year by or on behalf of an employer and must not have been charged to income-tax for any earlier tax year.

## Exceptions
* Any salary paid in advance that has already been included in the total income of a person for a tax year shall not be included again when the salary becomes due.
* Any salary, bonus, commission, or remuneration (by whatever name called) due to, or received by, a partner of a firm from the firm shall not be regarded as salary for the purposes of this section.

## Practical Example
*Note: The following amounts are illustrative assumptions.*
Suppose an employee is owed ₹5,00,000 as salary for the current tax year, which remains unpaid at the end of the year; under clause (1)(a), this is still taxable. If the same employee receives an advance salary of ₹1,00,000 for the next year during the current tax year, it is taxable in the current year under clause (1)(b). When that advance amount officially becomes due in the subsequent year, it is not taxed again pursuant to sub-section (3). If a firm pays a partner ₹2,00,000 as commission, that payment is not treated as salary under this section pursuant to sub-section (4).
